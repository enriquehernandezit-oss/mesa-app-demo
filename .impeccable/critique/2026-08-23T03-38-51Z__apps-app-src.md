## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**Not AI-generated — emphatically, and for a specific reason: the product thesis is legible in the pixels.** The score reveal (88px old-style brass numeral, "TU PUNTUACIÓN" eyebrow, your two list-neighbors, then your friends' scores on the same place) is a real editorial idea, not a template. The restaurant profile's three attributed circles (Tú / Amigos / Todo Mesa) is an original answer to "a place has no rating."

Checklist run honestly: **absent** — gradient text, glassmorphism-by-default, numbered section markers, side-stripe borders. **Present** — a hero-metric row on Rankings and *twice* on Profile (a 3-stat row plus a 2-card grid repeating "Rank en RD #1" verbatim); near-identical card grids in Activity (8 consecutive rows of the same sentence template); eyebrow density past diminishing returns (DESCUBRE / LISTAS DESTACADAS / TU LISTA / OCASIÓN / PUNTUACIONES / SUS PUNTUACIONES / …) — though `docs/DESIGN.md` licenses the eyebrow as a system decision, so that one is voice, not slop.

**The single strongest machine-made tell is icon incoherence.** A proper hand-drawn SVG family exists (`router.tsx:34-68` — 24-box, 1.6 stroke, outline, consistent) and is used *only* in the tab bar. Everywhere else the app grabs the nearest Unicode character: `◉ ▤ ➤` (QuickActions), `✓`, `◇ ♡ ✓` (three different families/weights in three adjacent ProfileTab rows), `⇅`, `≡`, `›`, plus two full-colour emoji — `🥂` (CheersButton.tsx:50) and `🔥` (RankingsTab.tsx:101). Those emoji are the only saturated non-brass colour in an app whose design law states "brass is the only accent." `▤` (U+25A4) has poor system-font coverage on iOS/Android and will render as tofu for some users, on the home screen.

**Deterministic scan** (`detect.mjs`): 5 findings on `apps/app/src`, `apps/app/index.html` clean. Four are one real token plus three uses of it (`--ease-spring`, all one-shot celebrations — `stamp-in`, `cheers-pop`, `score-settle`); the fifth (`broken-image` at CompareCard.tsx:35) is a regex false positive on multi-line JSX — verified at runtime that both images have real srcs and `naturalWidth: 1200`. **Net: the static detector found nothing real.**

**Overlay** (injection succeeded, ran on 11 routes × 2 themes, server stopped afterward): independently reproduced B's contrast numbers to within rounding, which cross-validates both measurement paths. Its `cream-palette` and `cramped-padding` hits are false positives against this project's spec (`#f5efe4` is the mandated Afternoon `--bg`; the flagged element is an explicitly-sized 327×44px flex row). Genuine new hits: `dark-glow` on `.tab-fab` and `gpt-thin-border-wide-shadow` on `.map-frame`.

## Overall Impression

The craft is real and the core loop is the best thing here — but this audit went deeper than the previous two (full persona stress-testing at phone width, both themes measured rather than inferred), and it surfaced a **P0 that the prior passes never touched: the ranking flow cannot survive a back-gesture or a reload.** That is the atomic unit of the entire product, and it silently loses work in a way that looks like ordinary abandonment in analytics.

The second theme is that **the default theme is the weaker one.** Afternoon (light paper, the default) has 7 distinct failing contrast pairs including a back button at 1.09:1 — effectively invisible — while the same control measures 14.89:1 in Candlelit. Contrast parity is inverted throughout: muted metadata passes comfortably in dark and fails in light. That is the signature of a system verified in one theme and assumed in the other.

Third: **two regressions shipped in the most recent commit** (`87e4ad7`, the craft pass). Both are called out as P1 below and both are self-inflicted rather than inherited.

The biggest opportunity is unglamorous: route the rank flow, fix the light theme, and finish the error-recovery work that was started but not completed. None of that is redesign; all of it is follow-through.

## What's Working

**1. Attribution enforced by the type system.** `ScoreAttribution` (patterns.tsx:70-74) is a required discriminated union, so "whose score is this?" is a *compile error* to omit. Turning a design law into a type is the right instinct and is rarely done. It works because the rule *is* the product — it's what stops Mesa becoming Yelp.

**2. Removal-with-undo instead of a confirm dialog.** `lib/rankingRemoval.ts` is a module rather than a hook specifically so the pending delete survives the row unmounting; it re-densifies positions *and* scores optimistically to match the server's own `rewrite`, so nothing reads stale during the undo window; it guards double-taps with a keyed `Set`; and the toast's timer is the single source of truth, pausing while the tab is hidden. Better than a confirm dialog and better than most production undo implementations.

**3. Loading and motion hygiene that survives measurement.** `FeedSkeleton` reconstructs the loaded layout's exact geometry so nothing reflows on arrival. B verified 75/75 focusable elements render a visible focus ring (4.11–4.63:1, passing the 3:1 non-text threshold) and that **zero** elements outrun the global `prefers-reduced-motion` reset. Zero console errors and zero failed requests across the full sweep.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] One back-gesture or reload destroys the entire ranking flow
**What.** All five steps — find spot → sentiment → compare → reveal → note — run at a single URL (`/rank`) driven purely by component state (`RankAPlace.tsx:88-97`). Verified live: from the sentiment step one `history.back()` landed on `/discover` with everything lost. The in-app "‹ Atrás" steps back correctly, so the platform gesture does something *completely different* from the same screen. Reload behaves identically.
**Why it matters.** This is the atomic loop the whole product is built on. A user four comparisons deep who edge-swipes on iOS, presses Android back, or gets a call loses everything — no warning, no draft, no "¿Descartar?". It suppresses the one behaviour Mesa depends on and is invisible in analytics because it looks like abandonment.
**Fix.** Give each step a real route (`/rank/find`, `/rank/:id/sentiment`, `/rank/:id/compare`, `/rank/:id/reveal`, `/rank/:id/note`) with search params carrying `sentiment`/`position`, so back is stepwise and reload resumes. Minimum viable: a back-guard once `sentiment !== null`, persisting in-flight state to `sessionStorage`.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] Two regressions from the last commit (`87e4ad7`)
**What.** (a) `PullToRefresh.tsx:52` now renders a **permanent 100.8px spacer** in normal document flow above the feed — the outer track is `height: maxHeight` unconditionally, and only the *inner* div is transformed off-screen. Previously the container was `height: pull` (0px at rest). That is 12% of a 375×812 viewport of dead space above the fold on the home screen, and because only `/discover` mounts the component, content visibly jumps ~100px when switching tabs. (b) The note-step `save` mutation (RankAPlace.tsx:178-199) has **no `onError`**, so a failed note save silently resets the button and discards the note. The commit message claimed error-recovery was extended to the mutations that lacked it; `saveNote` and `SavedRow.remove` got it, but the most important one in the flow did not.
**Why it matters.** (a) is the first thing every user sees. (b) is a silent data-loss path in the step immediately after the product's emotional peak — and the reveal step one screen earlier handles this correctly (`commitInitial.isError` → "No se pudo guardar este ranking." + "Reintentar"), so the inconsistency is within one file.
**Fix.** (a) Collapse the track to `height: pull` (or `max-height` transition) so at-rest height is 0 while keeping the transform-driven spinner. (b) Add `onError` to `save` with a retry toast, matching the pattern already used three times elsewhere in the same session's work.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

### [P1] The default theme fails contrast in seven distinct places
**What.** Measured in Afternoon (light, the default), worst first: `.resto-back` back-arrow **1.09:1**; `.resto-share` **2.94:1**; danger eyebrow + "Eliminar cuenta" **3.65:1**; inactive tab labels **4.03:1**; `--accent #9a6a28` on `--bg #f5efe4` **4.11:1** (the most widespread — eyebrows, scores, `.mesa-body`); `.resto-map--fallback` **4.29:1**; `--on-accent` on `--accent` **4.41:1**. Root cause for the first two: `.resto-back`/`.resto-share` take the *page* tokens (`--text`, `--accent-strong`) while sitting on a photo scrim; `.rail-card` (feed.css:345) correctly re-points `--text → --on-photo` for exactly this case, `.resto-photo` never did. In Candlelit those same two measure 14.89:1 and 10.0:1 — light-theme-only defects. Candlelit has its own smaller set (avatar initials 3.03–3.47:1, inactive tabs 3.78:1). Additionally the Afternoon toggle knob is **1.42:1** against its track and unselected chip borders are ~1.2:1 (WCAG 1.4.11 wants 3:1 for control boundaries).
**Why it matters.** A back button at 1.09:1 is invisible, and it's the only exit from a full-bleed photo screen. The `--accent`-on-`--bg` pair is a *token-level* failure, so it recurs everywhere rather than in one component.
**Fix.** Re-point `--text`/`--accent-strong` to the `--on-photo*` family inside `.resto-photo`. Darken Afternoon `--accent` (or use `--accent-strong` for small text on `--bg`) until the pair clears 4.5:1. Raise `--tab-inactive`. Give the toggle knob and chip borders a 3:1 boundary.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable colorize`

### [P1] Old-style figures make the app's own numbers look broken
**What.** Cormorant Garamond ships old-style (text) figures by default and nothing sets `lining-nums` — every `font-variant-numeric` in the codebase is `tabular-nums`/`proportional-nums`, which don't change figure style. On screen: the Rankings list reads **"I, 2, 3"** (position 1 renders as a Roman-looking I); Profile shows **"#I Rank en RD"** and **"o Seguidores"** (zero as a lowercase o); and on the score reveal the "9" descender collides with the restaurant name below (`line-height: 0.9` on 88px).
**Why it matters.** These are the app's own numbers — the substance of a ranking product — rendering as what looks like a font bug. Users won't diagnose an OpenType feature; they'll conclude the app is broken.
**Fix.** Add `font-variant-numeric: lining-nums` to serif consumers that display counts/positions (`.ranking-numeral`, `.lb-rank`, `.profile-stat__value`, `.stat__value`). Keep old-style on the score circles where it genuinely looks better, and raise `.rank-reveal__score` line-height from 0.9 to ~1.05.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable typeset`

### [P1] Scores are attributed structurally but not semantically
**What.** The type system guarantees an attribution *value*; it doesn't guarantee a human can read one. `badgeText()` (patterns.tsx:78-90) renders `kind: 'friends'` as **the bare count**, so Explore shows a large "8.8" with a small "2" beneath and no word anywhere explaining it — which reads as "8.8, 2 reviews." Separately, 18 score renders bypass `ScoreBadge` entirely via raw `displayScore()`; the worst is `RankAPlace.tsx:775`, the find-a-spot list at step one of the core loop, painting naked 9.1 / 9.6 / 8.7 circles with no label and no `aria-label` (a screen reader hears "eight point eight, two").
**Why it matters.** "A score is always attributed, never the place's own rating" is the product thesis, not a style rule. Every unattributed number teaches users that Mesa is a rating app after all.
**Fix.** Make `badgeText` return `"${count} amigos"` (or use `caption`, which `ScoreBadge` already supports and `RestaurantProfile` already uses correctly), add `aria-label` to the circle, and either route the bare call sites through `ScoreBadge` or lint-ban `displayScore(` outside `patterns.tsx`.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P2] Nothing gives the user a way out
Three findings, one root cause:
- **"Quitar" is visually identical to "Agregar nota" on touch.** `.link-action--danger` is styled *only* inside a `@media (hover: hover)` block (rankings.css:246-248) — on a phone there is no hover, so the destructive control and the benign one render identically, 40px apart, on all 42 rows.
- **All 5 `ErrorState` call sites are prose with no retry** (DiscoverTab.tsx:77, RankingsTab.tsx:139, ActivityScreen.tsx:99, ExploreScreen.tsx:130, MapScreen.tsx:109). Rankings has no pull-to-refresh either, so a failed load is a dead screen. `docs/DESIGN.md` explicitly forbids this ("pair with a concrete retry, not just this line").
- **The 404 is an unstyled English "Not Found"** at the top-left, under the notch, with no wordmark, tab bar, or link — and in a Capacitor shell there is no browser chrome to escape with.
**Fix.** Move `--danger` colour onto the base rule. Give `ErrorState` an `onRetry` prop and wire all five. Add a themed `notFoundComponent` with "Volver al feed."
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P2] Explore's 13-chip rail hides 70% of the app's filtering
**What.** One `ChipRail` holds sort + open-now + 4 price tiers + 7 neighborhoods — measured `scrollWidth: 964px` against ~343px visible, so **9 of 13 options sit off-screen** with `scrollbar-width: none` and no gradient or arrow affordance. Chips are 9px mono. The selected state is a 10% brass wash on white, so the selected chip reads as *less* prominent than the unselected ones — the inverse of the intended signal — and `Chip` never emits `aria-pressed` (its sibling `Toggle` gets `role="switch"`/`aria-checked` right).
**Why it matters.** Neighborhood is the single most important filter for a Santo Domingo discovery app targeting Piantini/Naco/Zona Colonial, and it is ~620px off-screen behind an invisible scroll.
**Fix.** Split into two labelled rows (Ordenar/Abierto/Precio | Barrio) or move neighborhood into a sheet behind a "Barrio" chip showing the current selection. Raise chips to 11px, give `--sm.--selected` the solid fill its `md` sibling has, and add `aria-pressed`.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

Three rapid taps on `.cmp-card` advanced three comparisons and landed on the reveal — the cards re-render in place with no transition lock, so an accidental double-tap answers a comparison the user never saw. Tapping the 5th occasion chip does nothing, with no message (`cur.length < 4`, RankAPlace.tsx:436). Ties report as wins: `a.score >= a.yourScore ? 'por encima de'` (ActivityScreen.tsx:167) produced "Carolina rankeó Boga Boga con 9.5 — por encima de tu 9.5" on screen. `/no-such-route` is an unstyled English dead end.

**Sam (accessibility) — gaps are inconsistencies, not ignorance.** The app has focus rings, reduced-motion, `aria-live` toasts and a correct `role="switch"` Toggle — which makes the misses stand out. **The vibe-note textarea has no label at all** (no `<label>`, no `id`, no `aria-label`, placeholder only — RankAPlace.tsx:416-422); this is the signature input of the product and a WCAG 3.3.2 failure. `Chip` never emits `aria-pressed`, so every filter in the app is stateless to a screen reader. Inactive tab labels fail contrast in **both** themes (3.98:1 / 3.78:1) at 10px — below the iOS 11pt floor, on 4 of 5 primary nav items. "No me convenció" measures 3.01:1 and *looks disabled* beside its two siblings. 49 map pins are `<g tabindex="0">` with no `role="button"` — 49 tab stops announcing as graphics with 27px hit areas.

**Casey (distracted mobile, one thumb).** 100.8px of permanent dead space at the top of the Feed (12% of the viewport, above the fold), with a visible ~100px jump when switching tabs. Sentiment and note screens put every control in the *top* third and leave the bottom half empty — the inverse of the thumb zone; same on Map (card ends at 400px with 400px of nothing below). ## Minor Observations

- `.sentiment--loved` carries a permanent brass border and brass text while `.sentiment--low` is muted grey (screens.css:155-161) — three peer options rendered as recommended / neutral / discouraged. For a product whose entire output is a ranking, this **biases the input data**, and the brass outline also reads as "already selected."
- **"Listo" and "Guardar nota" call the identical `save.mutate(position)`** (RankAPlace.tsx:384 and :464). The comment at :387 claims "Listo" finalizes *without* the note, but `note` is in the closure — it saves. Two buttons, one behaviour, contradictory labels. There is also no way to discard a typed note.
- `RestaurantProfile` offers "Rankear otra vez" (top) and "Rankear este spot" (bottom bar) on a place already ranked #1 — two labels, one action, one factually wrong.
- 140-char cap on the vibe note with **no character counter** — typing just stops.
- 96 interactive elements measure under 44×44: `.mesa-toggle` 42×25, "Eliminar cuenta" 42.5px tall, `.cheers` 39×44, `.tonight-card__name` 25.2px, `.activity-row__place` 16.5–34.7px, activity avatars 36×36, and 49 map pins at 24.6–30.9px. Adjacent-gap violations too: chip rails at 6.0px, action rails at 7.0px, and Rankings content abutting the fixed tab bar at **2.5–4.5px**.
- Sub-12px text is pervasive: 8px `.ph-tag`, 9.5px `.upill` and `.profile-since`, 9px chips, 10px tab labels, 11.5px `.chars__line` body text.
- **Desktop has no `max-width` anywhere** — `.tab-shell`/`.topbar`/`.tab-bar` all render at 1280px and feed-note body lines measure **179 characters** (target: 65–75ch).
- `.tab-fab`'s glow is `rgba(154,106,40,0.35)` — the *Afternoon* accent — in both themes, though the Candlelit FAB fill is `#c09050`.
- `--surface` (#2c1516) against `--bg` (#210104) is **1.31:1** in Candlelit; with the hairline at ~1.2:1, feed cards have effectively no visible boundary. Beautiful on a calibrated screen, gone outdoors.
- `.tonight-card__wholabel` clips 5px of text ("…Natalia est…"); the compare step transiently overflows the document by 2px during `battle-in`.
- `state="active"` is declared on `Chip` and used nowhere — dead API. `Chip state="selected" tabIndex={-1}` is used as a static display badge — a `<button>` that isn't one.
- Spanish nits: **"27 spots tus amigos han rankeado."** is missing *que*; `"${inFirst} está"` renders "Lucía está" (unfinished); "Quité Ajuala de tu lista" has the app narrating in first person, which no other string does. "Afternoon"/"Candlelit"/"EULA" remain English in an otherwise Spanish Settings screen.
- The two inert quick-actions carry `aria-disabled` but no `[data-stale]` visual treatment — screen-reader users get the signal and sighted users don't. The parity is backwards.
- `.tab-link` has no `:active` press state (the FAB does), and the active tab is signalled by brass colour alone — no weight or indicator change.

## Questions to Consider

1. **Should Activity exist in this form?** Eight rows of "por encima de tu 9.4" turns your friends' dinners into a scoreboard against you. The promise is "see where your friends rank theirs," not "see who beat you." What would it look like leading with *the place* and the note, mentioning the delta only when it's genuinely interesting?
2. **What if "Cerca" opened a list rather than that map?** The dot-plot communicates less than a sorted "spots near you" list would. Either commit to a real MapBox tile layer (the stack already names MapBox) or let "Cerca" be a filter.
3. **The pairwise flow is the best thing here and it's buried behind the worst screen.** What if step 1 were three or four large photo cards — "¿Fuiste a alguno de estos?" from recency/saved/nearby — with search as the escape hatch, rather than 40 near-identical rows?
4. **Does "Me encantó" need to look different from "Estuvo bien"?** If the three sentiments are peers routing to different comparison bands, styling them good/neutral/discouraged puts a thumb on the scale of your own data.
5. **Is Compartir really the one filled CTA the Rankings screen deserves?** Sharing currently outranks ranking, and the screen carries three filled brass shapes against a design law permitting one.
